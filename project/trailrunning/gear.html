The right kit is the kit that fits your route, your weather, and your body. Start simple; add only what the day calls for.

A useful starting point

The trail-day kit.

There is no universal packing list. Distance, remoteness, forecast, and local rules all change what belongs in your pack.

01

Trail shoes

Look for a secure heel, enough room for toes to spread, and traction suited to the surfaces you run. Fit matters more than a spec sheet; try shoes on with the socks you plan to wear.

Start here
02

Hydration packs and water

A handheld bottle can work for a short loop. A hydration vest may carry front bottles or a reservoir, plus pockets for a phone and small essentials. Check fit, bounce, capacity, and refill access; plan water around route length and conditions.

Bring enough
03

Navigation and GPS watches

Know the route before you start. A GPS watch can offer breadcrumb navigation, distance tracking, elevation data, and useful battery life for longer outings. Download maps offline when possible and carry a backup plan; a watch does not replace route knowledge.

Know the way
04

Layers for the forecast

Weather can shift quickly, especially at elevation. A light shell, sun protection, and a warm layer may be essential even when the trailhead feels comfortable. Check the full forecast, not just town conditions.

Check conditions
05

Sports nutrition and small essentials

For longer efforts, portable options such as gels, chews, or bars can make fueling easier. Try products during training and choose what sits well with you; ordinary familiar food works too. Add a small first-aid kit, identification, and a charged phone.
